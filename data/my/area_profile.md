## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Tax structure context**: Malaysia has never had an annual recurring tax on net wealth. Estate duty (inheritance tax) was abolished in 1991 under the Estate Duty (Repeal) Act 1991. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) introduced at 6% in April 2015 was repealed in September 2018 under the Pakatan Harapan government and replaced with the Sales and Services Tax (SST).
- **The Wealth Tax Debate**:
  - The debate over a wealth tax (*cukai kekayaan*) in Malaysia is driven by stark wealth inequality: according to Forbes 2024–2026 reports, Malaysia's top 50 richest tycoons hold over RM450 billion in collective assets, equivalent to the entire federal budget.
  - Successive governments (Pakatan Harapan under Mahathir Mohamad/Lim Guan Eng 2018–2020, Perikatan Nasional under Muhyiddin Yassin 2020–2021, Ismail Sabri Yaakob 2021–2022, and the Madani government under Anwar Ibrahim 2022–present) have formally resisted a net wealth tax, citing concerns over capital flight, asset valuation of illiquid family businesses, and the goal of positioning Malaysia as a regional wealth hub. Instead, the government introduced targeted proxy taxes: a 2% dividend tax on annual dividend income exceeding RM100,000 (introduced in Budget 2025/2024), capital gains tax on unlisted shares (effective March 2024), and high-value goods tax proposals.
  - **Proposals**:
    - **Parti Sosialis Malaysia (PSM)**: Longest-standing advocate. Authored a detailed graduated wealth tax proposal in its post-COVID recovery framework (July 2020) proposing a 2% rate on assets between RM1bn and RM2bn, graduating up to 10% on assets over RM30bn. Reaffirmed in party congress resolutions (August 2026), official central committee statements (Gandipan Nantha Gopalan, June 2025), and health campaign appeals (Dr. Cecilia Anthonysamy, April 2026).
    - **Parti Islam Se-Malaysia (PAS)**: The party's 71st Annual Muktamar (September 2025) unanimously adopted an economic resolution moved by representative Azhar Mohamad Ali (PAS Kawasan Batu) demanding a wealth tax (*cukai kekayaan*) on luxury asset holdings, multiple properties, and high-value wealth to relieve the tax burden on ordinary citizens. Backbench MPs Ahmad Saad Yahaya (PAS MP for Pokok Sena) and Mohd Khairuddin Aman Razali (PAS, former MP for Kuala Nerus) both argued in Dewan Rakyat budget debates for a 2.5% wealth tax aligned with the Islamic zakat principle of *zakat harta*.
    - **Parliamentary Backbenchers across Party Lines**:
      - Syed Saddiq Syed Abdul Rahman (MUDA MP for Muar, former Minister of Youth and Sports): Explicitly demanded in Dewan Rakyat on 16 December 2020 that the government introduce a wealth tax, inheritance tax, and capital gains tax on individuals with extraordinary wealth.
      - Tan Sri Shahrir Abdul Samad (veteran UMNO MP for Johor Bahru, former Federal Minister, BNBBC Chairman): Explicitly proposed a 2.5% wealth tax on stored assets in Dewan Rakyat on 16 October 2014, citing Islamic zakat and European wealth taxes in Norway and the Netherlands.
      - Datuk Mohd Hisamudin Yahaya (UMNO Senator, Dewan Negara): Proposed a global millionaire wealth tax in the Senate on 30 November 2023 to generate RM1 billion annually.
      - Hassan Abdul Karim (PKR MP for Pasir Gudang): Repeatedly demanded a wealth tax on multimillionaires and billionaires possessing assets above US$1m in Dewan Rakyat budget debates (October 2025).
      - Charles Santiago (DAP, former MP for Klang): Vocal campaigner for an annual 2% wealth tax on the richest billionaires to raise RM10 billion for public healthcare and education (May 2026).
    - **Think Tanks & Economists**:
      - Bait Al-Amanah: Published the comprehensive policy study *Saranan Cukai Kekayaan* (late 2018 / 2019) modeling a 0.5%–1.75% (or 1.0%–3.5%) progressive wealth tax on net assets exceeding RM10 million, based on the Spanish *Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio*.
      - Research for Social Advancement (REFSA): Published policy analysis by Ng Sze Fung advocating wealth taxation to broaden the fiscal base.
      - Prominent senior economists: Tan Sri Ramon Navaratnam (former Deputy Secretary-General of the Treasury) and Dr. Jomo Kwame Sundaram (Khazanah Research Institute) have both championed wealth taxes as necessary instruments to redress excessive inequality.
      - Civil society: Anil Netto (Aliran) and Dr. Kua Kia Soong (Suaram) have published extensively on wealth taxation models (Warren-style 2%–3% wealth taxes, graduated 1%–1.5% schemes).

## 3. Legislatures and where their records are
- **Parliament of Malaysia (Parlimen Malaysia)**:
  - Dewan Rakyat (House of Representatives) and Dewan Negara (Senate).
  - Hansards / Official Reports (*Penyata Rasmi*): Published as searchable PDFs on [parlimen.gov.my](https://www.parlimen.gov.my/hansard-dewan-rakyat.html).
  - Written Answers (*Jawapan Bertulis*): Published on [parlimen.gov.my/jawapan-lisan-dewan-rakyat.html](https://www.parlimen.gov.my).
  - Parliamentary Repository: [repositori.parlimen.gov.my](https://repositori.parlimen.gov.my).
- **State Legislative Assemblies (Dewan Undangan Negeri - DUN)**:
  - 13 state assemblies. While states do not have constitutional jurisdiction over direct wealth taxes (Federal List under the Ninth Schedule of the Federal Constitution), state politicians and ADUNs debate national economic policies and cost of living.

## 4. Parties
- **Parti Islam Se-Malaysia (PAS)**: Major opposition party with official national platform endorsement via unanimous Muktamar resolution (Sept 2025) and repeated Dewan Rakyat speeches by its MPs (Ahmad Saad Yahaya, Mohd Khairuddin Aman Razali).
- **Parti Sosialis Malaysia (PSM)**: Core ideological supporter with comprehensive platform adoption, alternative budgets, and campaign leadership (Michael Jeyakumar Devaraj, S. Arutchelvan, Gandipan Nantha Gopalan, Dr. Cecilia Anthonysamy).
- **MUDA (Malaysian United Democratic Alliance)**: Founder and MP Syed Saddiq Syed Abdul Rahman explicitly called for wealth tax alongside inheritance and capital gains taxes in Dewan Rakyat (Dec 2020).
- **Pakatan Harapan (PH)**: Coalition includes PKR, DAP, and Amanah. Party backbenchers (Hassan Abdul Karim of PKR, former MP Charles Santiago of DAP) have strongly pushed for a wealth tax, though the coalition leadership in government (Anwar Ibrahim, Lim Guan Eng, Liew Chin Tong) has opted for targeted dividend and capital gains taxes rather than a net wealth tax.
- **Barisan Nasional / UMNO**: While official party leadership adheres to federal fiscal caution, senior backbenchers (Tan Sri Shahrir Abdul Samad in 2014, Senator Datuk Mohd Hisamudin Yahaya in 2023) have formally championed wealth taxes in Parliament.

## 5. Government below national level
- **Structure**:
  - Federal level -> 13 States (*Negeri*) + 3 Federal Territories (*Wilayah Persekutuan*).
  - Local level -> 144 administrative districts (*Daerah*), functioning as administrative divisions under district offices (*Pejabat Daerah*) and local authorities (*Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan* - City Councils / Municipal Councils / District Councils).
- **Taxation powers**: Under the Ninth Schedule of the Federal Constitution of Malaysia, direct and indirect taxation (income tax, company tax, customs, excise) is strictly federal. States only collect land tax (*cukai tanah*), quit rent, and forest revenues; local authorities collect local assessment rates (*cukai pintu* / *cukai taksiran*). Therefore, any wealth tax is strictly a federal legislative matter, explaining why subnational advocates always lobby the federal Ministry of Finance and Parliament.
