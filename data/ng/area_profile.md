## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- Nigeria has **no federal annual net wealth tax** and **no inheritance or estate duty**: the Capital Transfer Tax Act (Decree No. 12 of 1979) was abrogated in the 1996 budget and dropped from the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004 ([Taxing the Rich: Nigerian Fair Tax Monitor Thematic Report, Oct 2024](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/Taxing%20the%20Rich%20Spread.pdf?VersionId=0nArswGanmdZOgSm4gvshQZvXqKBUU_8); [Emeka Ndu in Leadership, 23 Dec 2024](https://leadership.ng/the-need-to-reinstate-capital-transfer-tax-in-nigeria/)).
- **Nigeria Tax Act, 2025** (enacted 26 June 2025, taking effect January 2026) overhauled income tax, capital gains tax, VAT and development levies, raising the top Personal Income Tax marginal rate to 25% without introducing a net wealth tax or an inheritance/estate tax ([PLAC summary of the Tax Reform Bills](https://placng.org/Legist/understanding-the-tax-reform-bills/); [Leadership on Taiwo Oyedele's committee reforms](https://leadership.ng/inside-nigerias-new-tax-reform-system/)).
- **2014–2020 Federal Luxury Asset Tax Initiatives & Proposals:** Former Coordinating Minister for the Economy and Finance Minister Dr Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (`A0077`) introduced a 10% import surcharge on new private jets and luxury surcharges on yachts and luxury cars in the 2015 budget ([Daily Trust, 31 Dec 2014](https://dailytrust.com/private-jets-operators-await-tougher-times/)); Finance Minister Zainab Shamsuna Ahmed (`A0076`) announced special luxury taxes on private jets and yachts in December 2018 ([The Sun Nigeria, 26 Dec 2018](https://thesun.ng/yachts-private-jet-owners-to-pay-luxury-tax-from-2019/)); and former Vice President Atiku Abubakar (`A0071`) proposed a 15% luxury tax on private jets, luxury cars, jewellery, designer goods and first/business-class tickets of the super-wealthy to fund a Poverty Eradication Fund ([Vanguard, 22 Nov 2020](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2020/11/recession-expunge-non-essential-items-from-2021-budget-atiku-counsels-buhari/)).
- **2017–2026 Oxfam / CISLAC / TJNA / CARE Reports (`H0001`, `H0002`, `H0003`, `H0006`, `H0009`):** Beginning with *Inequality in Nigeria: Exploring the Drivers* ([Oxfam May 2017 PDF](https://www-cdn.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/file_attachments/cr-inequality-in-nigeria-170517-en.pdf)), Oxfam in Nigeria (`A0001`), CISLAC (`A0002`), Tax Justice and Governance Platform (`A0003`), CARE International in Nigeria (`A0012`), and Tax Justice Network Africa (`A0016`) published *Income and Wealth Inequality in Nigeria: Trends and Drivers* and *Taxing the Rich: Nigerian Fair Tax Monitor Thematic Report* on 15 October 2024, proposing a 1% annual net wealth tax above $1 million (raising ~$7.5bn/year) or tiered rates of 2% above $5m, 3%–5% above $50m and 5%–10% above $1bn (raising $2.4bn–$6.1bn/year), alongside reintroducing a federal inheritance tax above ₦50 million and establishing a Comprehensive Wealth Registry ([Inequality Study Policy Brief PDF](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/POLICY%20BRIEF%20-%20The%20Inequality%20Study%20-%20Oxfam%20Nigeria%20%5BWEB%5D.pdf?VersionId=l6qTvy6zHRQvw5qVoERbhbP_kJCD2DVe); [Taxing the Rich PDF](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/Taxing%20the%20Rich%20Spread.pdf?VersionId=0nArswGanmdZOgSm4gvshQZvXqKBUU_8); [Nigerian Tribune, 15 Oct 2024](https://tribuneonlineng.com/nigeria-can-generate-n4-59trn-from-taxing-4690-wealthiest-citizens-yearly-cislac/)).
- **2021–2026 Academic, Legal & Business Proposals (`H0004`, `H0005`, `H0007`, `H0008`):** Senior Advocate of Nigeria Ahmed Adeniyi Raji (`A0055`, [Daily Post, 24 Oct 2021](https://dailypost.ng/2021/10/24/lawyer-urges-fg-to-introduce-wealth-tax-to-reduce-poverty/)), economist Dr Nasir Aminu (`A0054`, [Sahara Reporters, 21 Jan 2022](https://saharareporters.com/2022/01/21/clinton-did-it-1993-why-cant-we-do-it-radical-approach-reduce-inequality-nigeria-dr-nasir)), and Chartered Accountant Chief Blakey Ijezie's Lagos conference (`A0031`–`A0034`, [Vanguard, 13 Mar 2024](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2024/03/blakeys-wealth-tax-conference/)) called for a federal annual net wealth tax; C&I Leasing Chairman Emeka Ndu (`A0056`, [Leadership, 23 Dec 2024](https://leadership.ng/the-need-to-reinstate-capital-transfer-tax-in-nigeria/)) and accounting scholars Abdulganiyu Babayanju and Rasheed Olatunji Animasaun (`A0057`–`A0058`, [IJNRD PDF, Apr 2024](https://www.ijnrd.org/papers/IJNRD2404097.pdf)) called for reinstating inheritance/Capital Transfer Tax; and the African Centre for Tax and Governance (`A0059`–`A0063`, [LoGRI, Nov 2025](https://logri.org/2025/11/property-tax-reform-in-nigeria-comparative-insights-from-three-states/)), African Cities Research Consortium (`A0037`–`A0040`, [ACRC, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.african-cities.org/equity-and-digitisation-in-the-property-tax-system-in-lagos-a-win-win-for-all/)), WAIFEM (`A0064`–`A0065`, [Leadership, Oct 2023](https://leadership.ng/tax-richs-property-to-boost-revenue-waifem-charges-govt/)), and BudgIT (`A0091`, [State of States 2025 PDF](https://budgit.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/StateofStates2025SEIIWEB.pdf)) advocated progressive property-wealth taxation on millionaires and landowners.
- **Polls:** No representative public opinion poll asking specifically about a net wealth tax or inheritance tax in Nigeria was found during R001–R002.

## 3. Legislatures and where their records are
- **National Assembly (Senate and House of Representatives):** Bills, order papers and votes & proceedings are published by the Policy and Legal Advocacy Centre ([PLAC](https://placng.org/Legist/understanding-the-tax-reform-bills/)). While no standalone federal net wealth tax bill has been introduced since 2010, Senate plenary and screening debates yielded Senator Adams Oshiomhole (`A0073`, [Vanguard, 9 Dec 2012](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2012/12/i-will-send-rich-tax-defaulters-to-jail-oshiomhole/)), Senator Jimoh Ibrahim (`A0074`, calling for the rich to pay more tax on their luxury assets, [The Punch, 4 Dec 2024](https://punchng.com/senate-passes-mtef-probes-nnpcl-over-n8-4tn-subsidy-funds/)), Senator Mohammed Ali Ndume (`A0075`, calling to tax rich individuals and corporations rather than the poor, [Daily Trust, 30 Oct 2024](https://dailytrust.com/ndume-to-fg-i-wont-support-tax-increase-let-the-poor-live/)), and ministerial nominee Rauf Aregbesola (`A0072`, pledging to pioneer "privilege taxes" on wealthy Nigerians, [Channels TV, 29 Jul 2019](https://www.channelstv.com/2019/07/29/im-going-to-pioneer-serious-taxation-for-wealthy-nigerians-aregbesola/)).

## 4. Parties
- **Socialist Party of Nigeria (SPN, `A0045`):** Advocates progressive taxation on luxury properties of the rich and super-rich and heavy taxation of big business (`A0046` Abiodun Bamigboye, [SPN statement, 17 Mar 2018](https://www.socialistnigeria.org/3558/2018/03/17/repeal-lagos-state-land-use-charge-law-2018/); [SPN statement, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.socialistnigeria.org/8422/2026/02/05/tinubus-tax-reform-is-an-agenda-to-increase-revenue-for-looting-and-servicing-the-opulent-lifestyle-of-corrupt-political-class/)).
- **All Progressives Congress (APC) & Peoples Democratic Party (PDP):** Individual figures from both parties — including former PDP Vice President Atiku Abubakar (`A0071`), PDP Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde (`A0050`), and APC Senators Adams Oshiomhole (`A0073`), Jimoh Ibrahim (`A0074`), Mohammed Ali Ndume (`A0075`), and former Minister Rauf Aregbesola (`A0072`) — have advocated luxury, property or wealth taxes on the rich, though neither party's national manifesto proposes an annual net wealth tax ([Vanguard, 22 Nov 2020](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2020/11/recession-expunge-non-essential-items-from-2021-budget-atiku-counsels-buhari/); [The Punch, 20 Oct 2026](https://punchng.com/2027-makinde-promises-tax-breaks-reaffirms-claim-on-unremitted-%E2%82%A6250bn-fund/)).

## 5. Government below national level
- Under Nigeria's 1999 Constitution, the federal government legislates on income, capital gains and stamp duties, while state internal revenue services (SIRS) collect personal income tax and state property/land charges ([PLAC](https://placng.org/Legist/understanding-the-tax-reform-bills/)).
- Local Government Areas (774 LGAs) are restricted by the Fourth Schedule of the 1999 Constitution to tenement rates, market/kiosk fees, motor park levies and radio/TV licences; they cannot enact a net wealth or inheritance tax ([Taxing the Rich PDF](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/nigeria.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/Taxing%20the%20Rich%20Spread.pdf?VersionId=0nArswGanmdZOgSm4gvshQZvXqKBUU_8)). Sampling across 23 LGAs in R001–R002 located national, regional and local actors in their HQ, constituency or university LGAs and confirmed that LGA councils do not pass wealth-tax motions.
