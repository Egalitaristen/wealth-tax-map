## 2. Wealth tax history and live proposals (2010 onwards)
- **Historical Background:** Indonesia previously had an explicit annual net wealth tax under the colonial-era *Ordonansi Pajak Kekayaan 1932* (Staatsblad 1932 No. 405), which was updated after independence by Law No. 24 of 1964 (*Undang-Undang Pajak Kekayaan 1964*). This law imposed an annual tax on the worldwide net wealth of individuals exceeding statutory exemption thresholds. It was abolished in the comprehensive 1983-1985 tax reform via Law No. 12 of 1985 on Land and Building Tax (*PBB*), replacing asset taxation with real property taxation and income taxation.
- **Contemporary Revival (2020–2026):**
  - **Civil Society and Think Tanks:** The campaign for a modern wealth tax (*pajak kekayaan*) gained substantial momentum in 2021–2022 during the COVID-19 pandemic. The **Forum Pajak Berkeadilan Indonesia (FPBI)** launched its foundational White Paper advocating progressive taxation of high-net-worth individuals.
  - **The PRAKARSA:** Published multiple seminal Policy Briefs:
    - *Policy Brief 35 (November 2022)*: *"Pajak Kekayaan: Menakar Potensi dan Peluang Implementasi di Indonesia"*, proposing an annual progressive net wealth tax of 1% to 2% on individuals with net wealth above IDR 140 billion (~USD 10 million), estimated to yield IDR 54 trillion to IDR 155.3 trillion.
    - *Policy Brief 36 (January 2023)*: *"Pajak Kekayaan: Peluang Integrasi Zakat dan Pajak Untuk Mengatasi Ketimpangan Sosial Ekonomi"*, exploring synergy between Islamic zakat (*zakat mal*) on accumulated wealth and a statutory wealth tax.
    - *Policy Brief 42 (October 2023)*: *"Pajak Kekayaan untuk Mengatasi Distribusi Beban Pajak yang Asimetris"*, positioning wealth taxation as an antidote to asymmetric indirect tax burdens across ASEAN (Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam).
  - **CELIOS (Center of Economic and Law Studies):** Released influential policy studies in 2024 and 2025, notably *"Dengan Hormat, Pejabat Negara: Jangan Menarik Pajak Seperti Berburu di Kebun Binatang"* (August 2025), proposing an annual 2% wealth tax on the 50 richest individuals in Indonesia to raise IDR 81.56 trillion per annum, as well as an expanded progressive levy on 22,000 High Net Worth Individuals.
  - **2024 Presidential Election:**
    - **Ganjar Pranowo (PDI-P):** Pledged to evaluate a wealth tax on the super-rich to maintain fiscal sustainability and fund social welfare, explicitly differentiating assets from consumption.
    - **Anies Baswedan & Muhaimin Iskandar (Koalisi Perubahan):** Championed fiscal justice through progressive wealth and asset redistribution (*pajak kekayaan orang super kaya*), with campaign economic directors Thomas Lembong and Handi Risza detailing proposals to broaden direct taxation.
  - **International Alignment:** Indonesian civil society and economists actively pushed for the Indonesian government to adopt the UN Tax Convention framework and the G20 2% billionaire minimum wealth tax proposal during the 2024 G20 discussions.
- **Public Opinion Polls:**
  - *CELIOS National Perception Survey (April 2026):* Found 89.77% public approval across Indonesia for a 2% annual wealth tax on the top 50 richest individuals to fund social protection and combat extreme inequality (reported by *Harian Kontan*).
  - *Earth4All / Ipsos Global Survey (June 2024):* Found 86% public support in Indonesia for establishing a wealth tax on the very wealthy to fund public health, education, and climate action (highest amongst surveyed G20 countries).
  - *Glocalities / Millionaires for Humanity Poll (May 2021):* Found 79% support among Indonesian respondents for a 1% annual wealth tax on fortunes above IDR 140 billion ($10 million).

## 4. Parties
- **Partai Demokrasi Indonesia Perjuangan (PDI-P):** Centre-left, social-democratic. Candidate Ganjar Pranowo explicitly proposed evaluating wealth taxes on the super-rich in 2024.
- **Partai Kebangkitan Bangsa (PKB):** Moderate Islamic, traditionalist. VP candidate Muhaimin Iskandar (*Cak Imin*) advocated progressive taxation of extreme wealth.
- **Partai Keadilan Sejahtera (PKS):** Islamic welfare and democratic party. DPP Head of Economy Handi Risza and parliamentary representatives officially urged serious consideration and implementation of net wealth taxes to boost the tax ratio and curb structural inequality.
- **Partai Buruh (Labour Party):** Left-wing, labour confederation party revived in 2021. Formally champions a 2% net wealth tax raising Rp 152 trillion per annum as the primary fiscal alternative to regressive VAT (PPN 12%) hikes and recurring tax amnesties.
- **Other Parties (Golkar, Gerindra, NasDem, PAN, Demokrat):** Tend to prioritise broadening personal income tax (PPh), voluntary disclosure programs (*Tax Amnesty* / *Program Pengungkapan Sukarela*), and consumption taxes (PPN 12%), though transition figures such as HIPMI Secretary General Anggawira (TKN Fanta) noted that wealth taxes on high-asset holders are an active policy consideration.

## 5. Government below national level
- **Structure:**
  - 34 Provinces (*Provinsi*) — regional level.
  - 514 Regencies and Cities (*416 Kabupaten, 98 Kota*) — local map level.
- **Taxing Powers (UU HKPD No. 1/2022):** Under Law No. 1 of 2022 on Financial Relations between the Central Government and Regional Administrations (*UU Hubungan Keuangan antara Pemerintah Pusat dan Pemerintahan Daerah / HKPD*), sub-national governments are constitutionally and legally restricted to:
  - Provinces: Motor vehicle tax (*PKB*), vehicle title transfer (*BBNKB*), heavy equipment tax (*PAB*), surface water tax.
  - Regencies / Cities: Rural and urban land/building tax (*PBB-P2*), land and building acquisition duty (*BPHTB*), goods and services tax (*PBJT*), underground water tax.
  - Sub-national governments **cannot** levy taxes on personal net wealth, capital assets, or corporate income. Therefore, all wealth tax advocacy at the regional or local level is directed at urging the national government and DPR RI to legislate.
- **Official Source for Geography Units:**
  - Badan Pusat Statistik (BPS) official administrative master codes, updated through UN OCHA COD-AB (licensed `CC-BY-IGO-3.0`).

## 6. Search terms
- **Primary Indonesian Terms:**
  - `pajak kekayaan` (wealth tax)
  - `pajak orang kaya` (tax on the rich)
  - `pajak orang super kaya` / `pajak kaum tajir` (super-rich tax)
  - `pajak atas aset bersih` (net asset tax)
  - `pajak individu berpenghasilan tinggi` / `HNWI`
  - `zakat dan pajak kekayaan` (synergy between zakat and wealth tax)
- **Ambiguous Terms (verify carefully):**
  - `pajak penghasilan` (*PPh* — direct income tax; excluded unless taxing unrealised gains or functioning as a net wealth proxy)
  - `pajak pertambahan nilai` (*PPN* — indirect consumption tax/VAT; excluded)
  - `pajak bumi dan bangunan` (*PBB* — property/real estate tax; excluded)
  - `PPnBM` (*Pajak Penjualan atas Barang Mewah* — luxury goods sales tax; excluded)
  - `tax amnesty` / `Program Pengungkapan Sukarela` (voluntary disclosure program without regular net wealth levy; excluded)

## 9. Known networks
- **Forum Pajak Berkeadilan Indonesia (FPBI):** National coalition for tax justice comprising civil society organisations, think tanks, and anti-corruption watchdogs.
- **The PRAKARSA:** Lead policy research institute driving wealth tax modeling and empirical ASEAN comparisons.
- **CELIOS (Center of Economic and Law Studies):** Prominent independent research institute proposing concrete 2% wealth tax schedules.
- **INDEF (Institute for Development of Economics and Finance):** Major economic research institute whose directors and researchers engage in fiscal equity debate.
- **Publish What You Pay (PWYP) Indonesia:** Natural resource and extractive industry fiscal transparency coalition.
- **Pusat Kajian Hukum dan Keadilan Sosial (PUSKAHA) FH UGM:** University legal centre advocating fiscal rights and wealth tax mechanisms.
- **Transparency International Indonesia (TII):** Anti-corruption body highlighting illicit asset accumulation and fiscal opacity.
- **Seknas FITRA (Forum Indonesia untuk Transparansi Anggaran):** Budget transparency network monitoring regional and national fiscal distribution.
- **Liga Mahasiswa Nasional untuk Demokrasi (LMND):** National progressive student organisation actively campaigning for wealth taxes on conglomerates.
- **Civil 20 (C20) Indonesia:** Official civil society engagement group of the G20 during Indonesia's 2022 presidency; issued summit communique demanding global wealth taxation and a UN Tax Convention.
- **Institute for Demographic and Poverty Studies (IDEAS):** Poverty research institute advocating wealth taxation on UHNWIs and conglomerates for fiscal justice.
- **Botax Consulting Indonesia:** Independent tax consultancy founded by former DJP auditors arguing for net wealth taxation to eliminate corporate profit-hoarding tax shelters.
