# Canada — area profile

## 1. Scope

R001, 24 October 2026: **seed**, national first pass, with Quebec/BC party sources and a seven-city search sample. Evidence window 2010 onwards; found evidence 2020–2026. English and French searched. This is an incomplete register of public support. See [quality review](qa_R001.md).

## 2. Parliamentary evidence and parties

The [16 November 2020 Commons motion](https://www.ourcommons.ca/Members/en/votes/43/2/22) proposed 1% on wealth above CAD20m alongside excess-profit taxation and spending commitments. It failed 27–292; all 27 Yeas are harvested, not the House as an institution. Later votes remain to be harvested.

The [PBO's 2025 election-costing landing page](https://www.pbo-dpb.ca/en/epc-estimates--estimations-cpe/45/EL-45-1023246-P) attributes an annual graduated wealth-tax proposal to the NDP: 1/2/3% above CAD10m/50m/100m. The costing agency is not an endorser. The [Green 2025 platform](https://cdn.greenparty.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/GP_Platform_English_FINAL-1.pdf) also specifies graduated net-wealth taxation.

## 3. Regional sources

[Québec solidaire's 3 September 2026 release](https://www.newswire.ca/fr/news-releases/quebec-solidaire-va-taxer-les-grandes-fortunes-pour-reparer-l-ecole-publique-887039790.html) names Ruba Ghazal and Alejandra Zaga Mendez and proposes a tax on fortunes above CAD25m. A Quebec May2025 study-motion roll call is queued as H0003; read the names in the official journal before adding members.

The [BC Greens' Fair Share Act FAQ](https://bcgreens.ca/the-fair-share-act-faq/) specifies a one-off levy and subsequent annual net-wealth tax. The [launch statement](https://bcgreens.ca/fair-share-act-would-unlock-22-2b-for-childcare-transit-and-housing/) supplies direct evidence for Emily Lowan and Emma Davis. Other provincial legislatures and party branches need later passes.

## 5. Geography and local decision

Official [Statistics Canada 2025 register and boundaries](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/08de9f4c-61f4-4b6d-8189-d17e3282d36d): 5,054 census subdivisions, 13 provincial/territorial parents. Subdivisions include municipalities and statistical equivalents, including reserves and unorganized territories; they are not all council jurisdictions. Official identifiers CSDUID and PRUID are used. [Local map service](https://geo.statcan.gc.ca/geo_wa/rest/services/2025/lcsd000a25s_e/MapServer) and [2021 province outlines](https://geo.statcan.gc.ca/geo_wa/rest/services/2021/Digital_boundary_files/MapServer/0) use EPSG3347. [Open Government Licence–Canada](https://open.canada.ca/en/open-government-licence-canada) permits reuse with attribution. Digital boundaries include water and Arctic territorial extents. `scripts/build_ca_geography.py` rebuilds the register/map and caches downloads outside the dataset.

Official-domain keyword samples for Toronto, Vancouver, Montréal, Ottawa, Calgary, Edmonton and Winnipeg found no qualifying municipal proponents. Property-tax results were insufficient. Decision: use aggregate campaigns and provincial sources before expanding city-by-city research. Seven units are `in_progress`; others are `pending`. No city or province is marked exhaustively searched. No claims about legal taxing powers are made in this run.

## 6. Search terms and exclusions

English: wealth tax, annual net wealth, billionaire tax, Fair Share Act, wealth levy. French: impôt sur la fortune, taxe sur la richesse, grandes fortunes, ultra-riches, Québec solidaire. Capital-gains inclusion, property/mansion taxes, inheritance and generic fair-share demands are insufficient alone. A costing does not establish personal advocacy by everyone named in it.
